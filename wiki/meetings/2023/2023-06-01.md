## Overview

Moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md) (co-moderators). TG2 approved raising the maximum fractional digits from 20 to 100 (#786), and agreed in spirit that UTC offset time zones (#788) are worthwhile but left the offset precision open ([FYT](../../people/FYT.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) wanted minutes, not nanoseconds). Two PRs (#783 sentence break suppressions, #780 locale-set stability note) needed more feedback from Apple and Mozilla. The group also discussed how to prevent misuse of localized strings after the NNBSP breakage on homedepot.com, and whether to make `Etc/Unknown` a first-class time zone, with no consensus. The notes write [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) as "YSK" in two places; this is normalised to [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md).

## Editor's Update

[RGN](../../people/RGN.md) landed the large change aligning ECMA-402 with UTS 35 (non-terminal references and use of vocabulary).

## Normative: raised maximum fractional digits from 20 to 100 #786

- proposal: [ecma402#786](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/786)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) recalled this was discussed in 2021-07; `Number.prototype.toFixed` already allows 100, and it is only 80 more digits on top of the 300+ in NumberFormat v3. [USA](../../people/USA.md) cited formatting cryptocurrencies as a use case. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) asked whether it is implementable and whether significant digits should change too; [USA](../../people/USA.md) answered that the consistency argument does not apply to significant digits. **Conclusion: TG2 approval.**

## Normative: Allow UTC offset time zones #788

- proposal: [ecma402#788](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/788)

[SBE](../../people/SBE.md) asked what this provides beyond the IANA `Etc/` aliases; [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) answered that those cover only large discrete chunks, and [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) added that offset strings are valid per the spec so they should be supported for interoperability. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) objected that the number of time zones goes from under 600 to a huge number, 99.999% useless, and that [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) can no longer index into a supported list; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) likewise could not see who would use it. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) countered that UTS 35 already has a locale-data-free algorithm for formatting arbitrary offsets (e.g. `GMT+7`). The debate turned on precision: ECMA-262 currently allows nanoseconds, [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said storage would have to grow for that, and [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) saw no use case below minutes, while [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) cited the pre-1909 Netherlands offset (hundredths of seconds) and interoperability with the IETF format and `DefaultTimeZone`. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) supported the spirit of the PR; [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) is willing to adjust precision and mainly wants to avoid an inconsistency between ECMA-262 and ECMA-402. **Conclusion: UTC offset time zones are good, but the precision needs more discussion; the group would agree to the PR once a suitable precision (maybe minutes, not nanoseconds) is chosen.**

## Editorial: Added note about sets of locales for web browser implementations needing to not change as a result of user behaviour #780

- proposal: [ecma402#780](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/780)

[DLM](../../people/DLM.md) will collect feedback from the Mozilla team. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) asked whether the "same version of the same host on the same platform" wording allows for explicit modes such as Apple's Lockdown Mode, which disables features that are fingerprinting vectors; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) argued that this counts as a different platform, and [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) agreed since Lockdown Mode is system-wide. [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) wrote the PR based on Apple and Mozilla feedback. **Conclusion: another round of feedback from Apple and Mozilla is needed, including on Addison's feedback.**

## Revisiting Normative: Allow UTC offset time zones #788

- proposal: [ecma402#788](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/788)

[FYT](../../people/FYT.md) reported that in the IETF spec the time zone precision is to the minute, not even seconds, unlike ECMA-262. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) will open a normative PR against ECMA-262 that same day; [Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md) will leave that part of ECMA-262 unaltered, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) noted this would fit in 16 bits. [SBE](../../people/SBE.md) raised iCalendar (UTC offsets down to the second, plus display names for zones outside IANA); [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) asked for a new issue to put on the agenda, and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) said the format should not leave iCalendar's needs unsatisfied. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) asked for evidence that offsets with non-zero seconds are actually used.

## How to prevent misuse of localized strings?

- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1KuIOSDQRliqCT3x3WX9Bg9H3hndfhAcH2G0aNoBKq18/edit)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) presented the homedepot.com incident, in which a CLDR update (the English NNBSP change) broke code that parsed and regex-rewrote localized strings; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) listed prevention options ([Temporal](../../proposals/temporal.md), better documentation, outreach, fuzz testing, longer canary incubation of CLDR updates) and long-term options (keep the hack, remove it, or limit the change to `en`/`en-US`). [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) asked how CLDR makes such decisions and favoured an English-only workaround for NNBSP; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) recalled Addison's opaque localized string proposal and floated a stable "international English" locale ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md)'s slides advise that developers who must parse should use "und"). [SBE](../../people/SBE.md) invoked Hyrum's law and said preserving deviations from the standard indefinitely is not tenable; [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) asked for a loud announcement when top-10 locales change, and noted the CLDR-to-ICU-to-Node communication gap. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md)'s stated takeaway: as stewards of web i18n, educate developers (stress fuzz testing, no introspection of Intl output) and engage more with CLDR about compatibility; a V8 colleague has started attending CLDR calls.

## Use Etc/Unknown (like ICU does) to handle the case when the OS's time zone is unrecognized by ECMAScript #25

- proposal: [proposal-canonical-tz#25](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-canonical-tz/pull/25)

[JGT](../../people/JGT.md) laid out four options for an unrecognised OS time zone (e.g. after Kiev/Kyiv): return `UTC` (non-Chromium), return `Etc/Unknown` (Chromium, cannot be used as input), also accept it as input, or throw. [SBE](../../people/SBE.md) said falling back to `UTC` is unusable for calendar contexts, which must know the zone is unknown. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) worried that `Etc/Unknown` is not in the IANA database and is rejected by other tools, and preferred `UTC` (or a separate way to detect the situation) over breaking pages; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) suggested platforms could also supply the current offset so an offset time zone could be the fallback, which [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) feared would hide the cause. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) asked whether this is really an implementation problem. **Conclusion: no firm conclusion; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md), [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [SBE](../../people/SBE.md) were identified as the people to follow up with.**

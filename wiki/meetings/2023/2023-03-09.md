## Overview

Co-moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md). Three discussion topics: canonical time zone identifiers ([JGT](../../people/JGT.md) for Temporal), the interaction of `Temporal.ZonedDateTime` with `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, and a long privacy-versus-i18n discussion on user preferences led by [BAN](../../people/BAN.md). [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [ZB](../../people/ZB.md) spoke but are not in the attendee list. In the notes `DM` ([DLM](../../people/DLM.md)), `HSJ` ([HJS](../../people/HJS.md)) and `RGT` (read as [RGN](../../people/RGN.md)) are typos for listed delegates.

## Canonical Time Zone Identifiers

- proposal: [proposal-temporal#2516](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-temporal/pull/2516)

[JGT](../../people/JGT.md) described the mess in time zone canonicalization: Firefox follows the tz database without its region merging, whereas V8 and WebKit use ICU/CLDR canonical IDs, which never change (so renames such as Saigon / Ho Chi Minh City, Kiev / Kyiv have not caught up). Temporal would make the differences more visible, so [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) proposed not canonicalizing TZIDs in JavaScript (only normalizing case) and listed options: switch everyone to CLDR; follow Firefox; hard-code the thirteen renamed names in Chrome and WebKit; or the status quo. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) preferred not to have ECMA-402 invent a canonicalization scheme (it should point to UTS 35), saw no reason for the work to block Temporal, and suggested a standalone proposal. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) said IANA already defines zones and links, so this is not a new scheme. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) explained that Safari uses the system ICU and updates time zones separately from ICU, making hard-coding hard; [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) thought a hard-coded list until an ICU API for the IANA canonical name exists was feasible, and [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) estimated renames happen about once a year. [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) offered to help champion a spec-text proposal and noted equality of `ZonedDateTime` objects should not change. **Conclusion: OK to entertain a proposal in this space.**

## ZonedDateTime / DateTimeFormat

- proposal: [proposal-temporal#2479](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-temporal/pull/2479)
- slide: [link](https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1b74GI-zHrG0wDzmwFs_yPWRli24KyVUNx3GeZt8JouA/edit#slide=id.g2175c57f0b8_0_29)

`Temporal.ZonedDateTime` is the first formattable object carrying its own time zone, which can conflict with the `timeZone` of an `Intl.DateTimeFormat`. The Temporal champions' approach is to throw if the `DateTimeFormat` was created with an explicit time zone (throwing on the shape of the input rather than on data), which requires `DateTimeFormat` to record whether the time zone was explicit, a one-bit change to the data model. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said this is not the first choice but is closest to a consensus among Temporal champions. [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) argued it need not affect `Date.prototype.toLocaleString` and that `DateTimeFormat` objects are meant to be reused, so the extra memory is an acceptable trade-off. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) said this sounded great. The notes record no formal conclusion.

## User Preferences Update

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md) reported that there is no consensus on a consent prompt (like camera and microphone) and that the focus has shifted to exposing preferences in ways that make it harder to individuate users, e.g. a small number of commonly used sets of deviations from one's locale rather than free choice of currency, separators or temperature units. Questions raised: how to choose such sets without being colonialist, whether arbitrary regions may be allowed, and the role of user research. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said 90-95% of users who deviate from their locale would be satisfied by a region plus one more set, that unit formatting is blocked on the lack of any way to find a user's unit preferences, and that the status quo forces every site to build its own solution. AVK questioned broadcasting such preferences globally and stressed privacy and protecting minority groups; [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) said the UX benefits do not clearly outweigh the privacy costs. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) preferred explicit user action (e.g. an HTML form element or picker) and implicit exposure only for critical items such as numbering system. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) asked for a list of realistic user stories and a classification of preferences by whether they are context-sensitive (temperature is not, traditional collation is language-bound). [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) worried about mismatches when the page language falls back (German preference, English page, miles). [RCA](../../people/RCA.md) stressed opt-in mechanisms. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) floated, and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) supported, supporting more sub-locales (variations of existing locales such as `en-US` with Celsius) as a path. [ZB](../../people/ZB.md) argued the long tail of preferences is a repeated "papercut" and that `en-001` already exists. [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) concluded there was enough information to proceed, with the group leaning towards focusing on commonly used sets; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said that was one proposal raised. The notes record no formal conclusion.

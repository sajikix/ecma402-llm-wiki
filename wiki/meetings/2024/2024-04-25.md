## Overview

Moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md) (co-moderators). TG2 backed the [DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md) refactor (#188) and agreed follow-ups (fix `useGrouping` for large numeric units, #192/#198), decided that normative PR #881 lands in the 2025 edition because the 2024 edition is in its opt-out period, and recommended [JGT](../../people/JGT.md)'s time zone ID requirements PR (#877) to TG1 subject to CLDR alignment and editor review. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) also raised objections to reducing `Accept-Language` for wider discussion. [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) spoke on #877 but is not in the attendee list; `PCO` in the notes is normalised to [PFC](../../people/PFC.md).

## Updates from the Editors

[USA](../../people/USA.md) reported that the spec freeze for the ES2024 cutoff had ended, so editorial PRs are merging again on a new `es2024` branch, with the opt-out period running until the ECMA GA in June. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) asked for a PDF preview; [USA](../../people/USA.md) will share it once produced and noted that clipped tables are a priority, since ECMA is looking for a better solution than manual CSS tricks.

## Updates from the MessageFormat Working Group

[EAO](../../people/EAO.md) announced the tech preview is live as UTS 35 section 9 and welcomed implementation feedback; the WG is working on the test suite and error handling. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) suggested approaching TC39-TG5 about MessageFormat. In response to [FYT](../../people/FYT.md), [USA](../../people/USA.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) confirmed the `Intl.MessageFormat` proposal repo already contains near-complete spec text, including user-defined functions as an optional formatter argument and a `formatToParts` representation specific to the ECMA-402 proposal; only `:time` was still missing.

## DurationFormat Update

- wiki: [Intl.DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-duration-format#188](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-duration-format/pull/188)

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md)'s refactor of `PartitionDurationFormatPattern` separates numeric and non-numeric formatting (`FormatNumericUnits` deferring to hours/minutes/seconds helpers). [USA](../../people/USA.md) thanked [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) and said the refactor makes the digital style's spec complexity acceptable. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) explained that the table-driven loop forces state tracking and unreachable branches, making implementation review hard; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) suggested splitting into three abstract operations with three tables (calendar units, hour/minute/second, subsecond), already the direction of the refactor. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) expected ICU4X work by a Summer of Code student starting in mid-May. **Conclusion: [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) to merge PR 188 promptly after at least one more review, and to investigate splitting the tables better (or inlining them into the abstract operations) to reduce unreachable branches.**

## Grouping separators in DurationFormat

- wiki: [Intl.DurationFormat](../../proposals/intl-durationformat.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-duration-format#192](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-duration-format/issues/192)

[FYT](../../people/FYT.md) asked what to do when digital-style hours, minutes or seconds are very large (e.g. `20:03:1,234,567`). [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) preferred leaving large-but-valid values as they are (Temporal is the tool for balancing) but changing the grouping separators, which is a small normative change setting an option on `NumberFormat`; [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md) agreed, and [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) noted that the subsecond handling does do some arithmetic. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) was fine with the change. **Conclusion: [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) to create a pull request, which will go to the next TG1 meeting for approval.**

## Normative: Canonicalize value of extension in ApplyUnicodeExtensionToTag #881

- proposal: [ecma402#881](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/881)

The earlier PR by [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) had been merged before TG1 approval, reverted, and never re-applied (the merge had closed the issue), until a contributor reapplied it; the PR has TG1 approval. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) would have liked it in 2024, but [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) pointed out that it is a normative change during the opt-out period. The group also discussed process fixes: open a PR whenever a revert is force-pushed to `main`, and re-open issues closed by a reverted change. **Conclusion: merge into 2025.**

## Normative: specify time zone ID requirements to reduce divergence between engines #877

- proposal: [ecma402#877](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/877)

[JGT](../../people/JGT.md)'s PR defines four rules for primary versus non-primary time zone IDs (IANA as the base with existing special cases, shared country code, following IANA's intra-country merges, and making about nine legacy POSIX primary IDs non-primary), with the aim of letting SpiderMonkey rely on ICU and CLDR. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) probed mismatches with CLDR's alias data for legacy IDs (e.g. `PST8PST`, `EST`, `MST`); [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) committed to aligning the PR's table with the existing web-compatible mappings and opening a CLDR PR to true up the BCP 47 data. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) largely supported it but argued the waiting period for swapping in renamed IDs should not be normative. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) found it fine, and [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) will work with [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) on test262 tests. **Conclusion: TG2 approves of the change as [JGT](../../people/JGT.md) described it, modulo alignment with CLDR legacy IDs and a more thorough review of the spec text by the editors.**

## I18N objections to reducing accept-language #10

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) used this item to raise awareness of a Chrome proposal to reduce fingerprinting by sending only the user's most preferred language in `Accept-Language` and moving to client-server negotiation, which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) considers harmful to i18n (Addison Phillips agrees). [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) noted it does not directly affect ECMA-402 and would like general guidance on well-defined, small sets of values; [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) questioned what the proposal targets and how widely multiple languages are used, while [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said negotiation is used and the `q` weights or long lists could be reconsidered. [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) will look into Safari's behaviour and discuss it next meeting. No TG2 position yet.

## Overview

Moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md) (co-moderators). The meeting was dominated by Intl Era Month Code design questions. TG2 agreed to accept and canonicalize calendar aliases, agreed that the spec should describe how every calendar's month and day numbering relates to ISO-8601, and endorsed the TG1 addendum to the text-directionality PR (`ltr`/`rtl`/`undefined`, no `ttb`). A long debate on what unqualified `-u-ca-islamic` should fall back to ended without a decision, though everyone agreed developers should not rely on it and engines should warn.

## Updates from the Editors

[USA](../../people/USA.md) presented for [RGN](../../people/RGN.md), who was initially absent: three editorial changes (a variable-markup/Oxford-comma convention fix from Anba, another editorial fix, and duplicate variants). [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) asked whether the spec was ready for the Ecma GA and [USA](../../people/USA.md) confirmed. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) later noted that `Intl.Locale.prototype.variants` has landed.

## Updates from the MessageFormat Working Group

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) reported a few big open questions on the `:unit`, `:datetime` and `:percent` formatting functions, with no agreement yet but hope of reaching some soon. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) also mentioned possible contributions to MessageFormat libraries from Bergen students in the fall, and that [LCA](../../people/LCA.md) might co-host.

## Updates from Implementers

[YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) reported that JSC landed an implementation of [proposal-intl-currency-display-choices](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-currency-display-choices) (the `formal` symbol), though it cannot be shipped. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) explained the proposal is still Stage 2 because only one currency (the Taiwan dollar) has a "formal" symbol in CLDR, and the underlying data quality is unclear. [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) had no updates.

## Behavior for calendar aliases.

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#11](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/11)

Some calendar names are now aliases for a longer canonical form ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md) cited Islamic and Ethiopic examples). The options were to accept and canonicalize (Firefox), accept without canonicalizing, or throw. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) pointed to `Intl.Locale`, which accepts and canonicalizes, and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) agreed; nobody advocated strongly for the second option. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) wants the proposal at Stage 2 soon, with only a couple of open issues. **Conclusion: Accept and canonicalize the aliases.**

## How to gracefully handle Hijri calendar fallbacks

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#29](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/29)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) proposed that unqualified `islamic` fall back on a best-effort, locale-dependent variant (e.g. `-tabular` for `en-US`, `-umalqura` for `ar-SA`), while Temporal, which has no locale, would require an explicit variant. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md), [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) objected: region-dependent results are unreliable and risk lock-in, a plausible-looking wrong date is the worst failure mode, and developers wanting Umm al-Qura can simply ask for it. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) argued for unconditionally mapping to `islamic-tbla`, matching what old Microsoft engines did for `-u-ca-islamic` on pages still online; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) put low weight on legacy IE behavior and wanted evidence from Microsoft. Falling back to the locale's default calendar was also floated and found acceptable by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md). TG2 agreed that unqualified `islamic` should not be relied on and that engines should be asked to log a console warning. No decision was made on the fallback itself (best-effort, TBLA, Umm al-Qura, or locale default); [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) suggested asking someone from Microsoft and continuing on GitHub.

## Temporal epoch years

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#33](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/33)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) sought agreement that the proposal defines the epoch for each calendar's `year` property instead of following the CLDR Extended Year, which differs from Temporal for the Chinese calendar. [USA](../../people/USA.md) supported it; [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) was unsure of the impact but had no objection. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said this is largely editorial and will keep everyone posted on the PR thread.

## Week numbering

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#15](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/15)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) sought agreement that, for now, only the ISO-8601 calendar supports week numbering (`weekOfYear` is `undefined` for e.g. `gregory`), since Gregorian has several week-numbering schemes; users convert explicitly with `withCalendar("iso8601")`. [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) supported it. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) argued that supporting ISO week numbering on Gregorian would be more ergonomic and suggested an `isoWeekOfYear` field; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said that would be additive and that returning `undefined` is more future-proof. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) was added as a reviewer.

## Gregorian-like calendars

- wiki: [Intl Era/Month Code](../../proposals/intl-era-month-code.md)
- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#2](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/2)

Should the proposal specify which calendars are Gregorian-like or leave that implementation-defined? [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) noted [ecma402#1003](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/issues/1003) and argued the spec should state that these calendars are proleptic with no Julian switch-over. [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) cautioned that ROC and Buddhist are not strictly derived from ISO-8601. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) preferred a table describing the expected behavior of every calendar rather than special-casing a few. **Conclusion: The spec should contain a high-level description of the algorithm for all calendars, so ROC, Buddhist, and Japanese will state that month and day numbers are the same as in ISO-8601.**

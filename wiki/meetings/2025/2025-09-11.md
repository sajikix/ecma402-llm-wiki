## Overview

The notes do not name a moderator. TG2 approved two normative PRs (#1026 `Intl.PluralRules` taking Intl mathematical values, and #1022 raising the Intl MV limits, subject to TG1 adjusting the bounds) and agreed that `overflow: "reject"` should be strictly rejecting for lunisolar leap months. Most of the meeting went to [HJS](../../people/HJS.md)'s "Standard Measures with U.S. English" explainer (`en-ZZ`), which drew discussion but no decision, and to how to restructure the NumberFormat rounding-mode editorial PR (#978). [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) spoke throughout but is not in the attendee list; the notes' PCO and HSI are normalised here to [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) and [HJS](../../people/HJS.md).

## Standard Measures with U.S. English

- proposal: [standard-measures-en-us](https://github.com/mozilla/explainers/blob/main/standard-measures-en-us.md)

[HJS](../../people/HJS.md) presented an explainer for a language tag with "U.S. English with standard measurements" semantics (currently `en-ZZ`), aimed at users who run en-US UIs but dislike US conventions, while avoiding the fingerprinting cost of granular overrides. Open risks are that no browser has tried exposing such a locale to the web, and that first day of week is not universally Monday. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) found the need real but preferred a more general mechanism and suggested `en-US-u-rg-001zzzz`, noting `ZZ` means "no region" in the Likely Subtags algorithm; [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) countered that a two-letter region is more web-compatible and asked for a memorable identifier, while [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) felt CLDR is the right venue. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) also pointed out engines could already ship such a locale without any ECMA-402 change. Next steps from [HJS](../../people/HJS.md): test web compatibility of the identifier, work out how the date-pattern override fits ICU/LDML, change the `001` `minDays` from 1 to 4, and research the heuristics for automatic promotion. No decision was recorded.

## Normative: Make Intl.PluralRules ResolvePlural and associated AOs take Intl mathematical values rather than Numbers #1026

- proposal: [ecma402#1026](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/1026)

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md) described this as an oversight: `PluralRules` should have used Intl mathematical values, so BigInt no longer throws (`ToNumber` throws on BigInt) and extra precision in strings is kept. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) noted it is a subset of the changes in [Intl Keep Trailing Zeros](../../proposals/intl-keep-trailing-zeros.md) and is happy for it to land. **Conclusion: TG2 approves ([RGN](../../people/RGN.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md), [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) +1).**

## Normative: Increase limits on Intl MV and explicitly limit significant digits #1022

- proposal: [ecma402#1022](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/1022)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) took the limits question to TG3 and will present slides at TG1 on whether the spec should define limits at all; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) may make the new bounds tighter than the current 100-fold increase (for example around 500 digits, exponents -1000 to +1000) and is not in a hurry to land it. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) was happy to take it to TG1, and [JMN](../../people/JMN.md) and [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) supported it, citing [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md) and [Decimal](../../proposals/decimal.md). **Conclusion: TG2 approves increasing the limits, with the exact bounds open to TG1's tweaks.**

## Add an overflow option that rejects on lunisolar leap months in common years #36

- proposal: [proposal-temporal-v2#36](https://github.com/js-temporal/proposal-temporal-v2/issues/36)

For Hebrew and Chinese calendars, adding a year to a leap-month date such as Adar I lands in a common year where that month does not exist. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) leaned towards keeping `overflow: "reject"` lenient here, but [HJS](../../people/HJS.md), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) argued that `reject` should be properly strict and the later tightening of a lenient rule would be a web-compat problem. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) agreed to the stricter behavior. This is a normative change against Intl Era Month Code, which reached Stage 2.7 with the lenient behavior, to be made as a small PR (by [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) or [SFC](../../people/SFC.md)) with tests, timing not urgent although one engine already ships.

## Allow engines to ship data for locales in Accept-Language #1010

- proposal: [ecma402#1010](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/issues/1010)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) raised an idea from the Web Engines Hackfest: an engine could make data for an extra language (for example Maori) available to sites only if the user selects it in `Accept-Language`, which is fingerprint-neutral. Detailed discussion was deferred to next month.

## Editorial: Simplify NumberFormat #978

- proposal: [ecma402#978](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/978)

[RGN](../../people/RGN.md) walked through the PR, which merges the two rounding-mode tables by adding a Negation column and drops `GetUnsignedRoundingMode`. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) still values the concept of an unsigned rounding mode for implementers and split the PR into three possible changes: merging the tables, inlining the AO, and passing the sign down. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) would do the first two, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) accepted the inlining once the tables are merged. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) noted much of this text will need to move to ECMA-262 for [Amount](../../proposals/amount.md), with a single shared operation. No formal conclusion, but the two sides converged on a smaller change.

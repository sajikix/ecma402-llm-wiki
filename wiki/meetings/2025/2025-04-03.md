## Overview

Co-moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md). Most of the meeting worked through open issues of the Intl Era and Month Codes proposal: `CE`/`BCE` was favoured for the Gregorian era codes (no universal consensus, to be presented at TG1), the ISO year was favoured as the extended year for the Chinese calendar, and codes were proposed for the Persian, Coptic, Minguo and Ethiopic calendars. On the `islamic`/`islamic-rgsa` calendars [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) put forward a proposed conclusion that [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) partly disputed. TG2 also agreed to add a test262 test for `Intl.Locale("und")`, saw further support for `Locale.prototype.getVariants` (#960) and heard an update on PluralRules `notation` (#989). [ZB](../../people/ZB.md) is listed but does not speak; [FYT](../../people/FYT.md), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), [EAO](../../people/EAO.md), [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) speak but are not in the attendee list.

## Era codes for the Gregorian calendar: AD/BC or CE/BCE

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#26](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/26)

After the champions agreed to base era codes on common English names, [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) asked whether the Gregorian era codes should be `AD`/`BC` or `CE`/`BCE`. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md), MGR, [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) favoured `CE`/`BCE` (a forward-looking, developer-facing identifier, in wide use in academic contexts and with no need to carry a religious connotation); [LAF](../../people/LAF.md), [PFC](../../people/PFC.md) and [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) stressed that either is fine as long as developers looking in devtools are not surprised. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) questioned why the group was moving away from CLDR's `gregory`/`gregory-inverse` codes, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) explained that the aim is to agree on what Temporal developers should see, then bring it to CLDR. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) summarised that there is no universal consensus, but `CE`/`BCE` has the stronger claim, and it will be presented at TG1.

## Extended year for Chinese: What is the epoch?

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#27](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/27)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) defined the extended year as the year expected when no era is given, and compared two frameworks: making each calendar's extended year follow the era of another calendar, versus anchoring each calendar in isolation (e.g. `AM` rather than `AA` for Ethiopic). [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), [HJS](../../people/HJS.md), MGR and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) preferred [SFC](../../people/SFC.md)'s per-calendar framework. For the Chinese calendar, which has no eras, [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) proposed using the ISO year as the extended year; [EAO](../../people/EAO.md), [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) and MGR gave +1. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) raised the Korean (Dangi) calendar, which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) had not yet researched, so that stays open.

## Era codes for the Coptic calendar

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#22](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/22)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) proposed `AM` for the positive era and a temporary code for the "Before Diocletian" era, which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) advocates removing (CLDR issue opened). [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) agreed the pre-era adds little, and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) suggested naming it something unappealing so that it does not stick, leading to "AM inverse" or `coptic-inverse`, in line with CLDR's "inverse" naming.

## Era codes for the Minguo calendar

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#24](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/24)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) found no established English convention and proposed transliterations ("Minguo" and "Minguo jian"). [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) pointed out that in Taiwan, where the calendar is used, "ROC" and "before ROC" are the common English forms and found a Taiwanese government page using them. MGR's framework (use the common English name if there is one, otherwise a scholarly/Latin name or a romanisation of the most common language) was endorsed by [HJS](../../people/HJS.md), and Wade-Giles was rejected as unhelpful for locals. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) accepted the "ROC" argument in principle if evidence appears; to be continued on the issue.

## Which Hijri calendars should Temporal support? #29

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#29](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/29)

Continuing from the previous meeting and the Temporal champions' discussion earlier that day: MGR and [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) argued that since Temporal has not shipped it can throw or drop these calendars while `Intl.DateTimeFormat` (which has shipped) falls back instead, whereas [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) said anything that resolves from `Intl.DateTimeFormat` should also not crash in Temporal. [PFC](../../people/PFC.md), [LAF](../../people/LAF.md) and [USA](../../people/USA.md) favoured keeping the identifiers recognised with a better fallback ([PFC](../../people/PFC.md): appearing to drop support for Saudi Arabia would be worse than a calendar that shows wrong dates), and [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) noted `islamic-rgsa` was never really implemented in any ICU and is an alias for `islamic`. [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) objected that remapping could be perceived as breaking and asked for evidence; [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) suggested searching HTTP Archive, and [DLM](../../people/DLM.md) will add a Firefox usage counter. MGR argued that guaranteeing Temporal stability would mean not supporting `umm-al-qura`, while [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) said revisions by its calendar steward should be treated like a time zone data update. **Proposed conclusion ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md)): calendar identifiers currently accepted in formatting must not start throwing; it is expected to be acceptable to remap the algorithms behind certain `islamic` variants in `Intl.DateTimeFormat` (with the remapped calendar visible in `resolvedOptions`) while remaining open to evidence that this would break the web; and it is therefore acceptable for Temporal to support only the three algorithms with some stability.** [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) asked about the vagueness of the third bullet (intentional for now), and [EAO](../../people/EAO.md) disagreed with the second bullet as a reversed burden of proof, so the wording is not agreed.

## Add test for Intl.Locale from "und" #4444

- proposal: [test262#4444](https://github.com/tc39/test262/issues/4444)

Chrome returns `undefined` rather than "und" (a fix from [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) exists), and Safari also returns `undefined`. SRL saw this as a test bug rather than a spec bug. [USA](../../people/USA.md), SRL and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) all agreed (+1) to adding a test.

## Normative: Add Locale.prototype.getVariants #960

- proposal: [ecma402#960](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/960)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) listed the two concerns from the last meeting: the return type and whether the API is well motivated. SRL explained that variants are not deprecated: they are used by language communities (e.g. Romansh idioms) and for regional styles where ISO is reluctant to assign a code, and CLDR accepts variant data. [USA](../../people/USA.md) added Basque, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) named micro-community variants and orthographic distinctions as use cases. This addresses the motivation question; the return type was not settled.

## Normative: add notation to PluralRules #989

- proposal: [ecma402#989](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/989)

[USA](../../people/USA.md) described a long-standing issue: `Intl.PluralRules` never takes the notation of a count, so `NumberFormat` and `PluralRules` can disagree, and the PR adds a `notation` option. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) expected web reality to match this and proposed taking it to TG1.

## Overview

Co-moderated by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (the notes file is named 2025-10-13 but its title reads 2025-10-09). The meeting cleared the last two open items of Intl Locale Info (an editorial note on text direction and a normative PR making lookups explicit) and agreed to ask TG1 for Stage 4. Most of the rest was Intl Era and MonthCode: alias removal aligned with CLDR 48, proleptic Thai Buddhist years before 1941, wording on the Japanese calendar authority and the Meiji start date, an agreed shape for the Hijri calendar fallback, and a long, unresolved debate about "safe" date ranges (1900 vs 1912 for the Chinese calendar). [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) also reported implementer news on `Intl.Locale` info getters becoming functions.

## Updates from Implementers

[YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) had no implementation updates beyond needing test262 links for some features on the Status Wiki; [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) will audit them. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) reported that the `Intl.Locale` info getters were changed to functions: Safari removed the old getter, Firefox has not yet, and V8 has approval to remove it (Chrome 143), with a kill switch as a safety net. PR 960 has tests but the wiki link is missing. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) had nothing from Mozilla.

## Updates from the W3C i18n Group

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) was on an email thread about W3C scraping GitHub updates; adding a `w3c` label to ECMA-402 proposal repos on an as-needed basis would be fine, while scraping all tc39 repos is out of scope.

## Intl Locale Info for Stage 4

- proposal: [ecma402#942](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/942)

[FYT](../../people/FYT.md) hopes to resolve the two remaining issues today (one editorial, one normative) and then go to TC39 for consensus on the normative PR and Stage 4.

## Editorial: Add note about inferring text direction from locale #103

- proposal: [proposal-intl-locale-info#103](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-locale-info/pull/103)

The PR adds an informative note that locale direction information can be a fallback when no metadata gives text direction. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) supported the note (while wondering if the getter should have been called `defaultDirection`). [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) objected to the last sentence, which points users to an API (string-meta) that does not exist in the platform, and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) questioned linking to a W3C group note that uses normative language. After discussing links to the W3C document, the group settled on the conservative option. **Conclusion: end the note at the comma and delete the "but rather" clause; [SFC](../../people/SFC.md), [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) agreed.**

## Normative: Add more explicit algorithms for lookups #92

- proposal: [proposal-intl-locale-info#92](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-locale-info/pull/92)

At anba's suggestion, [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) replaced the high-level "return the thing" wording with detailed lookup steps, which essentially say "do what ICU does". [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) supported merging, [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) confirmed it matches how ICU works, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) (who will read it before TG1) favours turning prose into steps as in intl-era-monthcode. **Conclusion: consensus that the PR is fine; reviewers are to give an explicit LGTM, then it goes to TG1.**

## Intl Locale Info for Stage 4

- proposal: [ecma402#942](https://github.com/tc39/ecma402/pull/942)

[YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) approved Stage 4 since the feature ships in Chrome, Edge and Safari. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) checked that test262 was updated after the getters-to-functions change; [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) and [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) confirmed both engines pass, and Safari already removed the getters. [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) already has a 30-minute TG1 agenda item. **Conclusion: TG2 supports Intl Locale Info for Stage 4 at TG1 ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md): "glad to finally graduate this").** [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) also asks one of the editors to sign off on the PR.

## Normative: Delete superfluous era aliases to align with CLDR #72

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#72](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/pull/72)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) explained that CLDR 48 keeps only one primary era name per calendar (plus `bc`/`bce` and `ad`/`ce` for Gregorian), so the extra invented aliases (for example `mundi` and `incar` for Coptic, and the alternates for ROC) should go. No web-compat risk since nobody ships them and ICU4X already rejects them. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) and [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) agreed. **Conclusion: TG2 approval, with [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) updating the PR to delete one leftover line.**

## Normative: Add wording for buddhist calendar complexities for pre-1941 dates #76

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#76](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/pull/76)

Thailand moved New Year from April 1 to January 1 in 1941. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) found that the JDK and .NET both extend the calendar proleptically without the April 1 New Year, and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) argued the only justification is compatibility with existing software, not a claim that pre-1941 accuracy is unimportant (a real third-party ICU bug report prompted this). [YSZ](../../people/YSZ.md) and [FYT](../../people/FYT.md) had no strong opinion. **Conclusion: approval of the proleptic behaviour; [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) will reword the PR to say it is for compatibility with other software before landing.**

## Editorial: Add calendar authority for Japanese Imperial era names #79

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#79](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/pull/79)

[BAN](../../people/BAN.md) asked who is the authority for era names versus their transliterations. [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) noted ECMA-402 would normally only recommend, and said the next area of focus will be what behaviour conforming implementations may or may not vary from CLDR. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) wanted tests for the era start dates given CLDR's history of bugs. **Conclusion: [BAN](../../people/BAN.md) will say only that era ranges are defined by the government of Japan and leave names out ([SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) +1).**

## Editorial: Be explicit about the Gregorian to Meiji switchover month and day in the Japanese calendar #86

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#86](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/86)

[HJS](../../people/HJS.md) asked to state ISO date 1868-10-23 as the Meiji start, since an incorrect CLDR date (a lunar date read as Gregorian) has spread widely. Starting Meiji at 1 January of Meiji 6 would be a valid design but a normative change, so both [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) and [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) preferred the smaller editorial fix. [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) agreed and thanked [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) for the research.

## Which Hijri calendars should Temporal support? #29

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#29](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/29)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md)'s revised conclusion: `islamic` resolves to `"islamic-tbla"` in `Intl.DateTimeFormat` (a warning may be printed), `Temporal` throws for `"islamic"`, `"islamic-umalqura"` must be supported and preserved, and every calendar returned by Intl must be accepted by Temporal. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) liked using a spec-defined fallback rather than an ILD one. **Conclusion: agreed by [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) and [HJS](../../people/HJS.md); follow-up normative PRs (in intl-era-monthcode and/or ECMA-402) to be approved at the November meeting so they can go to the Tokyo plenary.**

## Stage 2.7 review feedback: Be more specific about safe date ranges

- proposal: [proposal-intl-era-monthcode#64](https://github.com/tc39/proposal-intl-era-monthcode/issues/64)

[SFC](../../people/SFC.md) proposed a universal principle (matching ground truth back to roughly 1900, derived from the oldest living person and consistent with the 1900 MonthDay reference-year range), with exceptions for compatibility, new algorithms or data size. [HJS](../../people/HJS.md) and [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) preferred calendar-by-calendar ranges based on constraints (`islamic-umalqura` data spans 1882-2147; the Chinese algorithm starts in 1912; PMO data is freely available from 1900, with third-party data back to 1645 at about 1 KB). [RGN](../../people/RGN.md) argued accuracy is the overriding principle, with limits set by "size, trust, and effort", which [SFC](../../people/SFC.md) liked. No decision was recorded; the discussion was cut short.
